# Core-Finding

The draft core engine. Run it in step 1 of the `draft` entry point. Its
output, one defensible sentence, feeds the rest of the draft.

**Goal:** one sentence you can be wrong about. A topic is not a core. A theme
is not a core. A claim with a falsifiable opposite is.

## Four shovels

Dig with any of these until the sentence holds.

**Invert.** Flip the judgment. If the opposite is equally plausible, there is
real tension worth writing about. If not, the original is filler. The
disagreement is the core.

**Chase-the-premise.** Ask what unstated assumption the claim stands on.
Complete the sentence "True, but only if ___." The blank is often the real
argument.

**Chase-the-emotion.** Ask why the topic makes people uncomfortable, excited,
or confused. Discomfort usually marks a conflict hiding under surface
agreement. Name the conflict.

**Flip-the-definition.** Take the central noun and attack its assumed meaning.
The common definition is often what collapses first, and the collapsed
assumption is the core.

## The attack

One diagnostic question, run against the candidate core:

> **"If this is true, then why ______?"**

Fill the blank with the strongest counter-evidence: the fact that should not
exist if the core holds. If the blank cannot be filled, the core is too vague
to be wrong. Dig again.

## Stress-test gate

Three verdicts:

- **Holds.** The objection has a clean answer. Proceed to structure.
- **Morphs.** The objection reveals a sharper, more defensible version.
  Restart core-finding with the new candidate.
- **Breaks.** The core cannot survive. Replace it with a defensible
  alternative; do not paper over the break.

A morphed or broken core is the shovels working, not a failure. Restart until
the verdict is *holds*.
